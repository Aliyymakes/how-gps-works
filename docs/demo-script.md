# Demo video script

**Draft for Icarus.** Shot list for the short screen recording the brief asks us to submit (design.md section 1). About 2 minutes: 2:05 in total. Order follows the depth ladder (2a): the first screen's two-minute idea (the page's own three-step guide), then rung 2, then rung 3. Rung 4 (satellite clock drift) is on the page but not in this script; the issue names rungs 2 and 3 only. An optional rung 4 shot is at the end of this file. The page has four parts, each labelled "Part N of 4".

Rules for this file:
- Every button label and on-screen sentence in quotes is copied word for word from `index.html`, `web/first-screen.js`, `web/illustrations.js`, `web/step-geometry.js`, `web/step-signal.js`, `web/step-relativity.js` or `web/bridge.js`.
- The voice-over lines are drafts, not page text.
- Numbers on screen: 520.3 m and 0 m on the first screen were computed by replaying the Python with the default satellites (`web/config.js`) and the clock at 1.00 µs. The page shows metres through one rule (`web/format.js`: one decimal under 100 m, whole metres from 100 m), so 520.3 m appears as "520 m". The numbers replayed in Python for rung 3 are 14 chips (the real delay), a score of 1.00 at that shift, and 16.2 times for the best score against the runner-up with no noise. The other numbers the page shows (DOP and position error in rung 2; the match score at each shift in rung 3) are computed live and were not read from a run, so they are not quoted: the voice-over points at the readouts instead.
- The timing is an estimate from reading the code (how long each sentence takes to read aloud, plus the 450 ms glide when the lines meet). It has not been recorded or timed in a browser. Record on a warm cache: the first visit can take 10 s to 45 s to load (docs/ux-audit.md; the page itself says "The first visit can take up to a minute on a phone." while it loads), and the opening shot below assumes the page is ready in about 3 s (gotchas.md, 2026-11-24).

## What the page does at shot 9 (from `web/step-signal.js`)

The sentence and the badge change by level: "Found", "Barely found", "Lost". The sentences are "The noise is bigger than the signal, yet the match still finds it.", "The match still points to the right place, but only just." and "The phone can no longer find where the signal starts." With the page's settings (PRN 7, real delay 14 chips, seed 1), a Python replay of `signal.make_received` and `signal.correlate` at every notch of the slider (steps of 0.5) gives "Found" up to "7.0× the signal", "Barely found" first at "7.5× the signal" and "Lost" first at "12.0× the signal", which is the slider's last notch. So "Lost" shows only at the very end: drag all the way right. At 12.0 the close-up chart says "The phone's best match is now at shift 713, outside this close-up." (best shift 713 in the replay). Pause at about 7.5× and then at the end. Re-run for the final page; not yet seen in a browser.

## Optional shot 10: rung 4, satellite clock drift (about 15 s, not in the 2:05)

From `web/step-relativity.js`. Scroll to "Part 4 of 4" and the heading "Do the satellites' own clocks matter?" (the link line before it ends "Therefore let us see whether that drift can move the position."). The slider "Time since the satellite clocks were last corrected" starts at "24 h", so the effect is already showing, and the hint is "Drag this back to 0 and the two clocks agree. Drag it forward and watch the gap grow." The user drags the slider to "0 h" and back to "24 h". With the default satellites and the section's phone clock error of 0, running the mechanism gives: at 0 h the sentence is "No gap yet. Both maps agree."; at 24 h the first card shows "Clock on the ground" 24:00:00.000 000 and "Clock on the satellite" 24:00:00.000 038, "Satellite clock ahead by" "about 38 µs" and "So every measured distance is wrong by" "about 11 km" (11,392 m). In the second card the map "The phone trusts its clock" reads "about 20 km off" ("Fix is far off"; 19,767 m), the map "The phone solves for its clock" reads "0 m off" ("Good fix"), and the sentence is "Every satellite is off by the same amount. To the phone that looks exactly like its own clock being wrong, and the fourth satellite already fixes that." Voice-over (draft): "The satellites' clocks drift too, by the same amount for all of them. A phone that trusts its clock is thrown off by kilometres. A phone that solves for its clock is not."
